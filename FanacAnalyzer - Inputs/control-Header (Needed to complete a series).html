Fanzines Needed to Complete a Series

These are the fanzine series on Fanac.org whose issues are numbered in a way which runs in order, but with issues missing from the run. Each series is followed by the numbers we do not have, and then by a count of the issues its numbering does account for, so that the two add up to the highest number we hold: Obsessions has 26 and wants 3, which makes 29. A series is taken to have begun at 1, so issues missing ahead of the earliest one we hold are counted along with the gaps inside; issues after the last one we hold are not, there being no way to tell from the page how far the series actually ran. An issue whose number is not a plain number, such as a 12.5, is passed over altogether: it is neither missing, nor does it fill a gap, nor is it one of the issues counted.

A series with no whole numbers which is instead numbered by volume and issue is listed the same way, with its missing issues written V1.3 for volume 1, issue 3. There each volume is taken to have begun at its issue 1, and nothing is counted past the last issue of a volume we hold, since how many issues a volume ran to is not something the page tells us.

A page with no Whole column which numbers its issues without numbering them within volumes is numbering them straight through, so its Number column is read exactly as a Whole column is read and its missing issues are listed as plain numbers. Where such a page leaves the Number cell blank on an issue whose name says which issue it is -- Sanders does on its "Sanders 1", the Planet on its "The Planet 1" -- the number in the name is used, so that the report does not ask for an issue the page plainly lists.

Where a page carries more than the fanzine it is about -- a supplement, a bound volume, or an issue of some closely-related fanzine -- those issues are numbered in their own right, and their numbers read as faults in the numbering of the series. Where setting aside the issues not named for the series leaves numbering which runs in order, the series is read without them: the page for Obsessions, which carries a single issue of Subsessions, is read as Obsessions alone.
